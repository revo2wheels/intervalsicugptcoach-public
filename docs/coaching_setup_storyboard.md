# Montis Coaching 101 — Setup Storyboard

## Purpose

Create a short, human video that shows how a coach connects athletes to Montis with an invite link, and how the athlete stays in control of what they share.

The story follows:

- **Anna Weber**, an endurance coach who runs **Anna Weber Coaching**.
- **Lena Brandt**, one of her athletes, a cyclist training for the Gran Fondo Wachau.

Anna invites Lena with one link. Lena opens it on her phone, signs in with Intervals.icu and accepts. From then on Anna can coach Lena through Montis, and Lena can stop sharing at any time.

This is the first coaching video. The second, **Montis Coaching 101 — Tools**, shows what Anna does once her athletes are connected.

## Format

- **Target runtime:** 4:00–4:20
- **Style:** warm, simple, step by step, coach-first
- **Narration:** one presenter/avatar, conversational
- **Visual language:** real Montis screens (linked in each scene), with light motion and callouts
- **Primary message:** one link per athlete; the athlete agrees to exactly what is shared, and can stop at any time

## The story in one sentence

Anna creates an invite link, Lena accepts it, and both of them can see and control the connection from the Montis hub.

## Screens

Every screen uses made-up people and example data: the coach Anna Weber Coaching (annaweber.example) and the athletes Lena Brandt, Marco Rossi, Sofia Lind, Tom Berger and Sam Carter. Links are relative to this `docs` folder.

# Storyboard

## Scene 0 — Coaching with Montis

**Time:** 0:00–0:25

**Visual**

Anna at her desk. Five athlete cards float around her laptop: Lena, Marco, Sofia, Tom and Sam. Each card sits on its own Intervals.icu account. A single link joins each athlete to Anna's Montis coaching.

**Voiceover — presenter read**

> This is Anna. She coaches five endurance athletes, and every one of them trains with their own Intervals.icu account. Montis lets Anna coach all of them in one place, without taking over anyone's account. It starts with one simple thing: an invite link.

**On-screen text**

`Your athletes keep Intervals.icu. You coach them through Montis.`

## Scene 1 — Who needs what

**Time:** 0:25–0:45

**Visual**

A two-column card: **Coach** and **Athlete**.

| | Coach | Athlete |
|---|---|---|
| Montis membership | Montis Subscriber | None — free |
| Intervals.icu account | Yes | Yes — a free account is fine |
| Email | Intervals.icu email matches the membership email | Nothing to match |

**Voiceover**

> Only the coach needs a membership. Anna has a Montis Subscriber membership, and her Intervals.icu email matches her membership email, so Montis can find it. Her athletes use Montis for free.

**On-screen text**

`Only the coach pays. Athletes use Montis free.`

**Production note**

A one-off Supporter payment does not include coaching. Don't show prices in the video.

# HeyGen build notes

## Presenter treatment

- Use the same presenter as Montis 101, so the series feels like one course.
- Show Anna and Lena as illustrated or lightweight cinematic characters, not extra talking avatars.
- Use the real screens for every step; animate the pointer and the highlight, not the interface itself.

## Keep the narration human

The whole video answers three plain questions:

1. How does a coach invite an athlete?
2. What exactly does the athlete agree to?
3. How does each side stay in control?

## Product guardrails

Do not say or imply that:

- Montis sends the invite link or messages athletes;
- an invite link works for more than one athlete, or forever;
- athletes can see each other or the coach's list;
- the athlete gives Montis their Intervals.icu password;
- athletes need a paid membership;
- a Supporter payment includes coaching.

Use these facts instead:

- one link, one athlete, 14 days; the coach sends it;
- the athlete signs in on Intervals.icu itself and accepts on the invite page;
- the invite page lists exactly what the coach can do;
- either side can end the sharing at any time;
- Intervals.icu stays the source of truth.
