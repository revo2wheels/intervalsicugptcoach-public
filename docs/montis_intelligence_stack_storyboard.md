# Montis Intelligence Stack — Janet & John Storyboard

## Purpose

Create a clear, human video that explains how Montis turns athlete data into a governed coaching decision.

The story follows:

- **Janet**, an endurance athlete preparing for a Sunday event.
- **John**, her coach, who uses an AI connected to Montis.

Janet sees the result in the Montis app. John asks an AI connected to Montis about the same athlete. Both receive the same governed coaching truth because both experiences use the Montis Coaching Engine and five-layer Intelligence Stack.

## Format

- **Target runtime:** 5:55–6:15
- **Style:** warm, simple, visual, athlete-first
- **Narration:** one presenter/avatar, conversational rather than technical
- **Visual language:** one continuous left-to-right journey; Montis tier colours remain consistent
- **Technical detail:** visible in labels and side panels, while the narration stays plain
- **Primary message:** the AI explains the coaching decision; it does not invent it

## The story in one sentence

Janet asks, “What should I do today?” Montis checks what she did, how her body responded, how her performance behaved, whether she is adapting, and where she is in her plan—then gives Janet and John one explainable decision.

# Storyboard

## Scene 0 — What is Montis?

**Time:** 0:00–0:35

**Visual**

Open on an athlete surrounded by disconnected charts, scores and notifications. The noise recedes as the Montis mark appears. The data reorganises into five coloured layers and then resolves into one clear coaching direction.

**Voiceover — presenter read**

> Endurance athletes collect more data than ever, but data alone does not tell them what to do next. Montis is an endurance coaching intelligence system. It connects to an athlete’s Intervals.icu account and brings together training, wellness, performance, adaptation and calendar context. Its evidence-led sports-science engine turns that evidence into one governed, explainable coaching decision. Montis does not ask an AI to invent the answer. Montis resolves the answer first; the app or a connected AI then helps the athlete understand it, question it and act on it.

**On-screen text**

`Montis turns endurance data into governed coaching decisions.`

`Adapt · Train · Evolve`

**Transition**

The five coloured layers move aside to reveal Janet preparing for her event.

## Scene 4 — Tier 1: What stress was applied?

**Time:** 1:35–2:05

**Colour:** orange

**Visual**

Janet’s last seven days appear as training blocks. Fitness, fatigue and form lines move across the week. A second, quieter background band shows longer-term context.

**Voiceover**

> Tier One is Training Load. It asks: “What stress was applied?” Montis checks Janet’s recent volume, intensity and training load against her established capacity and recent baseline. It does not assume that more training is automatically better.

**Inputs shown**

- completed activities for actual load, plus the planned calendar for forecast context;
- duration, distance, sport and training load;
- intensity and intensity distribution;
- CTL, ATL and TSB;
- recent load direction, monotony and strain context;
- seven-day detailed evidence with broader chronic context where required.

**Outputs shown**

- total and sport-specific load;
- fitness, fatigue and form context;
- current load pattern;
- load pressure and load direction;
- intensity-distribution context;
- forward load trajectory.

**Janet example**

`Load pattern: BALANCED`

**Simple translation**

`Janet has trained enough to create a stimulus, without a clear load alarm.`

## Scene 5 — Tier 2: How is the body responding?

**Time:** 2:05–2:35

**Colour:** green

**Visual**

A simple body outline appears beside small trend lines for HRV, resting HR and sleep. Janet taps a short subjective check-in. Avoid presenting any single signal as a verdict.

**Voiceover**

> Tier Two is Physiology Response. It asks: “How is Janet’s body responding to that stress?” HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, form and Janet’s own check-in are read together and against her personal history. One poor night does not become a diagnosis, and a missing signal does not become an invented one.

**Inputs shown**

- longitudinal HRV and HRV ratio/trend;
- resting-heart-rate baseline and delta;
- sleep duration and score where available;
- current TSB and load pressure;
- latest daily subjective fatigue, stress, soreness, mood, motivation, injury and hydration;
- optional recovery observations and signal-quality context.

**Outputs shown**

- physiology state and meaning;
- autonomic/recovery status;
- HRV and resting-HR guardrails;
- sleep and subjective context;
- evidence coverage and confidence;
- a supporting recovery signal for downstream decisions.

**Janet example**

`Physiology reserve: WATCH`

**Simple translation**

`Janet is broadly coping, but a few recovery signals deserve attention.`

## Scene 6 — Tier 3: How does performance behave under stress?

**Time:** 2:35–3:15

**Colour:** blue

**Visual**

Show Janet riding:

1. a hard effort depletes a small “high-intensity battery”;
2. a long ride compares power with heart-rate drift;
3. hard days cluster on a seven-day strip.

Label these three lenses `WDRM`, `ISDM`, `NDLI`, then immediately translate them.

**Voiceover**

> Tier Three is Performance Intelligence. It asks: “How is Janet’s fitness behaving when stress is applied?” Montis looks at three things: how deeply she uses her finite high-intensity reserve, whether her aerobic efficiency survives long sessions, and whether hard work is being packed too densely.

**Inputs shown**

- W′ and rolling W′ context;
- maximum W′-balance depletion and joules above FTP;
- activity decoupling and moving time;
- Intensity Factor, Efficiency Factor and Variability Index;
- high-intensity session density;
- environmental context such as heat where available.

**Outputs shown**

- **WDRM:** maximum/mean depletion, moderate and deep-depletion counts, work above FTP and divergence context;
- **ISDM:** mean/max decoupling, high-drift count, long-session exposure and durability state;
- **NDLI:** rolling high-intensity work, hard-day count, mean IF/EF/VI and density context;
- consolidated operational state: `load_accepting` or `recovery_priority`.

**Janet example**

`Cardiovascular strain: HEAT INDUCED`

`Durability: STABLE`

`Operational state: LOAD ACCEPTING`

**Simple translation**

`The heat raised Janet’s cardiovascular cost, but her endurance remained stable and there is no major operational stop signal.`

## Scene 7 — Tier 4: Is the training actually working?

**Time:** 3:15–3:50

**Colour:** purple

**Visual**

Two power-duration curves appear: `previous window` and `current window`. Highlight 1 minute, 5 minutes, 20 minutes and 60 minutes. Show a curve rotating rather than simply moving up or down.

**Voiceover**

> Tier Four is Adaptation Progression, called ESPE. Load tells Montis what Janet did. ESPE asks whether it worked. It compares equivalent power-curve windows across the energy systems, so one personal best cannot pretend to represent the whole athlete.

**Inputs shown**

- equivalent current and previous rolling power-curve windows;
- 5-second context plus 1-, 5-, 20- and 60-minute anchors;
- CP, W′, pMax and FTP where available;
- regression slope, R² and curve-quality evidence;
- valid cycling power, or running power when supported.

**Outputs shown**

- percentage change at each anchor;
- anaerobic, VO₂, threshold and aerobic-durability status;
- vertical shift and curve rotation;
- adaptation bias and adaptation state;
- plateau detection;
- curve profile and system guidance;
- model quality and confidence context.

**Janet example**

`Adaptation profile: VO₂ / THRESHOLD DECLINE`

`Fatigue resistance: LIMITED, BUT IMPROVING`

**Simple translation**

`Janet’s recent high-aerobic performance is lower, while her ability to hold performance deep into a ride is improving.`

**Boundary callout**

`A decline is evidence to interpret—not proof of fatigue by itself.`

## Scene 8 — Tier 5: What can Janet tolerate now?

**Time:** 3:50–4:17

**Colour:** red

**Visual**

The first four coloured streams converge into a large ADE dial. The dial is labelled `CAN`.

**Voiceover**

> Tier Five is the Adaptive Decision Engine. First it answers “Can”: what can Janet tolerate now? It combines the operational state, risk and fatigue forecast, load direction, HRV guardrail, adaptation state, event context and sufficiently confident nutrition context.

**Inputs shown**

- Tier 1 load state and planned-load trend;
- Tier 2 physiological guardrails;
- Tier 3 operational state and risk context;
- Tier 4 ESPE adaptation state;
- future fatigue forecast;
- event priority, proximity and taper status;
- nutrition context only when confidence is adequate.

**Base outputs shown**

- ADE base score;
- immediate risk classification;
- capacity state;
- base directive—what the athlete **can** tolerate.

**Janet example**

`ADE base score: 92 / 100`

`CAN: Maintain training structure`

## Scene 9 — Can is not the same as should

**Time:** 4:17–4:40

**Visual**

The ADE dial remains visible. A calendar and Sunday event now slide over it. The word `CAN` stays on the left; `SHOULD` appears on the right.

**Voiceover**

> But what Janet can tolerate is not always what she should do. Her phase, recovery needs, event timing, taper and projected event form now govern the strategy. Janet may be capable of more work, but with Sunday approaching, the correct choice is to protect freshness.

**Governance inputs shown**

- current and projected phase;
- fatigue streak and recovery/deload requirement;
- planned load direction;
- taper requirement;
- days to target event;
- projected event TSB and target form range;
- controlled sharpening when appropriate.

**Final outputs shown**

- final governed directive;
- phase alignment;
- event/taper instruction;
- resolution state: `honoured`, `honoured_with_sharpening`, `overridden_by_phase` or `historical_only`;
- reasons, safeguards and future actions.

**Janet example**

`CAN: Maintain training structure`

`SHOULD: Taper—prioritise freshness and maintain low load`

`Phase alignment: ALIGNED`

`Resolution state: OVERRIDDEN_BY_PHASE`

**Production note**

`ALIGNED` describes the taper plan’s alignment with the required phase. `OVERRIDDEN_BY_PHASE` records that phase governance changed the ADE base directive. They are related, but they are not the same field.

# End-to-end product shot

Use this sequence for the animation. Keep infrastructure and protocol names out of the spoken story:

```text
Athlete or coach request
        ↓
Montis app or AI connected to Montis
        ↓
Secure Montis athlete connection
        ↓
Intervals.icu training, wellness, settings and calendar evidence
        ↓
Montis Coaching Engine
        ↓
Evidence validation and consistent data windows
        ↓
Five-layer Montis Intelligence Stack
        ↓
Training Load → Physiology Response → Performance Intelligence
        ↓
Adaptation Progression → Adaptive Decision Engine
        ↓
CAN → phase and event governance → SHOULD
        ↓
Montis governed report
        ↓
Montis app / Intelligence Lab / connected AI explanation
```

## Important distinction

The internal software tiers (`Tier-0` through `Tier-3`) are implementation and validation stages. They are not the same as the five athlete-facing Intelligence Stack tiers. The video should use the five product tiers for the main story and reserve the software execution chain for one architecture scene.

# HeyGen build notes

## Presenter treatment

- Use one presenter for continuity.
- Let Janet and John appear as illustrated or lightweight cinematic characters rather than additional talking avatars.
- Keep the presenter in frame for the opening, tier transitions and conclusion.
- Use full-screen motion graphics for the architecture and five tiers.

## Visual consistency

- Tier 1: orange — load/stress.
- Tier 2: green — physiology/recovery.
- Tier 3: blue — performance behaviour.
- Tier 4: purple — adaptation/progression.
- Tier 5: red — decision/governance.
- Montis Coaching Engine: the protected evidence-led calculation layer, not a conversational AI.
- Intelligence Lab: the athlete-facing place to explore the result.
- Intelligence Stack: the five governed coaching layers.
- Governed report: a clean structured package.
- AI/App: two presentation windows receiving the same package.

## Keep the narration human

Use the plain questions repeatedly:

1. What stress was applied?
2. How is the body responding?
3. How does performance behave under stress?
4. Is the training actually working?
5. What can the athlete tolerate—and what should the athlete do?

The acronyms should appear visually, but the presenter should translate them immediately.

## Scientific and product guardrails

Do not say or imply that:

- one metric determines readiness;
- NDLI directly measures central nervous system fatigue;
- a whole-activity DFA-α1 average is a measured LT1 or a Montis readiness score;
- Montis directly measures muscle glycogen;
- a falling power anchor proves fatigue by itself;
- missing evidence is estimated to create a complete-looking result;
- the LLM computes the athlete state or can override ADE;
- an AI client receives the athlete’s Intervals.icu token;
- a calendar change happens without an explicit tool action and confirmation.

Use these principles instead:

- evidence is combined, not isolated;
- reduced evidence reduces confidence or scope;
- capacity and strategy are separate;
- interfaces can change while the governed execution truth remains the same;
- the AI explains the result and supports dialogue;
- Montis owns the physiological interpretation and coaching decision.
